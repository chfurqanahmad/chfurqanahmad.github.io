Prospect Research & Outreach Workflow

How a research and outreach engagement moves from audience definition to qualified prospect research, personalized communication and organized follow-up.

This page describes the service approach and deliverables. Campaign scope and success measures are agreed before work begins.

1. Define the audience

Agree on the service being promoted, target geography, company profile, decision-maker roles and exclusions. For recruitment firms, employer acquisition and candidate sourcing are scoped separately.

2. Research and qualify

Check company activity, relevant needs, current contact roles and public contact routes. Record source links and review dates. An open job, expansion announcement or other signal is a reason to investigate, not proof of buying intent.

3. Review a sample

Share a small set of researched accounts for feedback before expanding the list. Resolve mismatches in fit, seniority or timing and check against your existing clients, contacts and exclusion list.

4. Prepare and coordinate outreach

Develop concise LinkedIn and email messages around a relevant reason to connect. Agree on messaging, sending responsibilities, follow-up timing and the rules for stopping contact before launching.

5. Track replies and hand over

Maintain contact history, next actions and reply status. Stop follow-ups when someone declines or asks not to be contacted. Pass interested replies to your team with qualification notes and conversation context.

What you receive

  • A structured prospect list with fit notes, sources and contact routes.
  • Personalization notes and agreed message drafts.
  • A CRM or spreadsheet with contact history, status and next actions.
  • A progress summary covering research quality, replies, handoffs and lessons for the next cycle.

How we assess progress

Agree on what makes a prospect qualified and a reply useful. Review accepted research, relevant conversations and handoffs alongside activity. Meetings and sales depend on audience fit, offer, timing and the sales process; they are not guaranteed.
